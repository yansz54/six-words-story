# Six Words, One Story — Method

## The entry

> Grandfather's watch stopped. So did he.

Six words, 39 characters — inside the 1–60 character gate.

## Why this story wins

The judge weighs, in order: (1) a complete story in exactly six words, (2) a
turn, surprise, or whole life implied, (3) every word earning its place,
(4) clean reading aloud, (5) something left unsaid.

- **Complete story (1):** a death is a full arc — a life, its end, and the
  object that outlived it. Beginning, middle, and end are all implied in one
  image.
- **Turn (2):** "stopped" does double duty. The watch stops, and so does the
  man. The surprise lands in the final three words, which re-read the first
  three.
- **Every word earns its place (3):** "Grandfather's" sets the relationship
  and the age; "watch" is the object that carries the metaphor; "stopped" is
  the hinge; "So did he" is the turn. No word can be swapped out without
  losing something.
- **Reads cleanly (4):** two parallel three-word sentences, a 3+3 beat that
  reads aloud without a stumble.
- **Unsaid (5):** the reader fills in the grandfather's whole life from one
  stopped watch — who he was, how he lived, who is left holding the watch.

## How it was picked

25 candidates drafted (22 valid six-word stories, 3 disqualified for wrong
word count). Compared pairwise against the judge's published criteria. The
winner beat its closest rivals — "He bought flowers weekly. She'd moved."
(no parallel rhythm, no double-meaning turn), "He forgave her. She never
knew." (flatter aloud, spells its situation out more), "She baked his
favorite cake. Alone." (a single scene, not a full arc), and "The letter
arrived fifty years late." (states its surprise instead of landing it).
